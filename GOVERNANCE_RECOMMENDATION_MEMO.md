**To:** Chief Data Officer / Data Governance Manager

**Re:** Recommendation to adopt the 8-layer Data Quality Governance Framework

Across our 13 tracked datasets, data quality issues today surface as ~36 raw control failures a day with no severity ranking, no trend signal, and no assigned owner, so problems are either missed in the noise or discovered only after they've already caused downstream harm. We recommend adopting the 8-layer governance framework already built and running in this warehouse — Detect (30 daily controls) → Assess (severity-weighted exception classification) → Prioritize (a 3-signal early-warning watchlist tracking null, duplicate, and control-failure trends) → Remediate (severity-based SLA ticketing) → Govern (a single 0–100 Data Trust Score per domain and enterprise-wide) → Improve (a governance maturity score) — because it converts that daily noise into a short, owned, trending list instead of a flood of undifferentiated alerts. Projected impact, computed from the current warehouse state: **100% of the 42 currently open data quality exceptions occurred in datasets the watchlist had already flagged (Watchlist or Monitor status) before the exception was logged — zero exceptions originated from an unmonitored "Clear" dataset**, meaning the early-warning layer is closing blind spots rather than reacting after the fact; **resolved remediation tickets close in a mean of 1.5 days**, giving a concrete baseline to hold future SLA performance against (21 of 42 open tickets currently carry an SLA breach flag, so the immediate opportunity is enforcement, not detection); and **removing the current watchlist/monitor penalty entirely would lift the enterprise Data Trust Score from 90.6 to approximately 93.9** — a real but modest ~3.3-point ceiling on what closing every open issue could buy today, not a tier change (both endpoints remain "Trusted"), which is the honest ceiling of this specific metric rather than an inflated claim. Ownership sits with dataset stewards for remediation execution, domain leads for trust-score accountability, and the CDO for enterprise-level maturity tracking. We recommend a phased rollout: pilot on the 4 Regulatory Critical datasets first, validate SLA enforcement and false-positive rates over one full reporting cycle, then extend to the remaining 9 datasets.

## Why the 90.6 "Trusted" Score Doesn't Mean This Is Low-Urgency

Before reading the 90.6 enterprise Data Trust Score as reassurance, it needs context: two of the four components behind the related Governance Maturity score — `control_coverage` and `audit_completeness` — are currently hardcoded constants (both fixed at 100.0), not measured signals, so they cannot reflect deterioration even when it's actively occurring. And the Trust Score itself has a concrete, verified failure case: **Risk Exposure (DS-003) failed 100% of its 150 control tests over the full 30-day window — every test, every day — yet its Risk domain still scores 94.1, "Trusted,"** because the formula averages continuous control effectiveness (94.8–95.1%, just under each rule's threshold) rather than counting binary pass/fail outcomes. Recomputing every domain's score under a simpler `(pass_count/total_tests)×100` compliance rate instead of the current weighted formula surfaces the same gap more broadly: Risk (94.1 "Trusted") and Product (96.9 "Trusted") both show **0.0%** simple pass/fail compliance; Customer (93.8 "Trusted") shows 13.6%. Neither formula is objectively correct — the current one tolerates near-miss thresholds and factors in trend data the simple version discards, while the simple version is more alarming but ignores early-warning signal entirely — but the gap between what either formula says and what the other says is exactly why this is urgent now, before the number is relied on for a board-level decision. A demonstrated fix (a binary floor capping any 100%-fail dataset at "At Risk") is detailed in the Priority Stack below and executed directly against live data in `notebooks/01_governance_analysis.ipynb` Section 6b, flipping Risk's score from 94.1 to 0.0 under the corrected rule.

## Regulatory Context

The 13 datasets in scope include regulatory-sensitive data (Customer Master, Transaction Ledger, Risk Exposure, General Ledger — the four "Regulatory Critical" datasets). The framework's four control categories plausibly map to specific BCBS 239 (Basel Committee, risk data aggregation) principles: Completeness and Accuracy controls support Principle 3 (Accuracy and Integrity); Consistency controls support Principle 2 (Data Architecture) by testing cross-dataset referential relationships; Timeliness controls support Principle 5 (Timeliness). **This is a plausible mapping to name, not a claim of regulatory validation** — a full gap analysis against BCBS 239's 14 principles, done in consultation with Compliance, would be the next step to formalize it. See `INTERVIEW_PREP_OBJECTIONS.md` Q14 for the existing disclaimer this is consistent with.

## Priority Stack

Given everything found in this hardening pass, here is the recommended fix order, ranked by leverage:

1. **Fix the Trust Score binary floor** (~1 day) — highest ROI, because every other governance output (the Scorecard below, domain rankings, board reporting) inherits its credibility from this one score. Demonstrated in `notebooks/01_governance_analysis.ipynb` Section 6b.
2. **Add failure-streak escalation** (~1 week) — 25 dataset-rule pairs have failed every day of the 30-day window, 22 of them carrying only 1 remediation ticket each; a rule failing 7+ consecutive days should auto-escalate one severity tier. Demonstrated in the notebook Section 6c.
3. **Require 24-hour acknowledgment of High-severity tickets** (~1 day, process not code) — data shows only 13.3% of High tickets are ever worked at all, vs. 31.6% of Critical, despite High's looser 3-day SLA. The gap is attention, not speed.
4. **Recalibrate SLA thresholds given the Critical/High breach inversion** — requires operational input from the teams actually working tickets, not a pure code fix.
5. **Damp trend percentages on near-zero baselines** (~1 day) — DS-012's duplicate rate moved 0.129 percentage points (0.0108% → 0.14%) and was reported as a "1192.9% increase," the loudest alert on the watchlist. Percentage-change against a near-zero baseline is inherently unstable; the fix is a minimum-absolute-movement threshold before a trend signal is allowed to fire, so operationally trivial moves stop generating severe-sounding alerts that erode trust in the watchlist over time.

## Governance Health Scorecard

What "working" means, three months after rollout, measured against the real baselines already verified in this warehouse (not round guesses):

- **Control pass rate:** from 20.1% toward 85%. *(Caveat: 85% is the dashboard's existing illustrative reference line, not a sourced regulatory benchmark — Tier 1 regulatory datasets likely warrant a tighter target, e.g. 95%+, while Tier 3 operational datasets may reasonably sit lower; tier-differentiated targets should be set with Compliance, not assumed uniform.)*
- **Mean time-to-remediation:** hold at or improve on the current 1.5-day baseline for resolved tickets — the real lever is getting more tickets *to* resolution, not making resolution faster.
- **Watchlist count:** from 8/13 toward a materially lower number, tracked alongside false-positive rate (see meta-governance below) so a falling count reflects real improvement, not threshold fatigue.
- **Trust score stability:** a >5-point swing between consecutive pipeline runs without a documented operational change is itself a signal the scoring formula needs the binary-floor fix above, not that the underlying data actually changed that much.

**Meta-governance — what happens when the framework's own signals become unreliable:** if more than 20% of Watchlist alerts are dismissed as false positives within 24 hours of being raised (the DS-012 failure mode), the zero-baseline threshold needs recalibration before more alerts are raised, not just the individual alert dismissed — disable and recalibrate before re-enabling. Reviewed on the same monthly cadence as the standard scorecard above, via the existing Layer 8 Monthly Governance Review, not a new mechanism.

**Framework evolution:** the control rulebook and its thresholds should be reviewed annually against regulatory updates (BCBS 239 revisions, new guidance) and against observed false-positive rates (DS-012 as the reference case), with new control types added as data assets expand — for example, ML model governance would need accuracy-drift and concept-drift controls not currently in scope.
